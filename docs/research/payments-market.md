# Brazilian Payments Reward a Ledger-First PSP

*Research report for the Jupiter project (a Go payments backend). Research date: 2026-09-29. Sources: seven internal research notes on market structure, Brazilian regulation and receivables, Pix/boleto/CNAB specifications, card rails, PSP API design, engineering writeups and open-source repositories.*

A payment service provider (PSP) is a thin API over a thick settlement and accounting machine, and in Brazil that machine is unusually heavy. Card sales to merchants settle over months: credit settles at D+30 per installment, and interest-free installments were **42.6% of card purchases in 2025**. Each future installment is a registered, tradable asset (a *unidade de recebíveis*, or UR) at a central registry. Since **11 May 2026**, every sub-acquirer must also pay its merchants through the centralized settlement system run by Núclea. Pix, meanwhile, has overtaken credit cards in Brazilian e-commerce (**42% vs 40%** by value in 2025) and brings its own public specification: an OpenAPI spec at **v2.10.0**, mTLS with certificate-bound OAuth tokens, a fraud-return mechanism (MED 2.0) and recurring payments (Pix Automático). Global PSPs such as Stripe and Adyen supply the API grammar Jupiter should copy: intent objects, idempotency keys, signed webhooks and an append-only balance ledger. They have no model for installment receivables, registry "effects" or anticipation, and Stripe's Brazilian accounts cannot use Pix Automático or refund boletos. Engineering writeups from Stripe, Square, Uber, Modern Treasury and TigerBeetle agree on one core: an immutable, zero-sum, double-entry ledger with pending and posted balances, invariant checks that run all the time, and an explicit "unknown outcome" state. Open source covers ledgers and ISO 8583 well (moov-io/iso8583 in Go). It barely covers Brazilian rails. The survey found no maintained Go CNAB library, no maintained SPI/DICT simulator, and no open 3DS2 ACS or card-network simulator. The result is a clear mandate: build a universal PSP core with Stripe-grade API conventions on a Postgres ledger, and put Brazilian rails (Pix, boleto/CNAB, card receivables and the registry, Núclea SLC) behind adapters talking to high-fidelity simulators. Where authoritative artifacts exist, reuse them, such as the bacen/pix-api spec and moov-io/iso8583.

## A 0.48-point spread pushes Brazilian acquiring toward credit and float

### Roles, message flow and money flow

A card payment has five parties. The cardholder's **issuer** extends credit. The **scheme** (*bandeira*) sets rules and nets clearing positions between issuers and acquirers. The **acquirer** (*credenciador*) holds the merchant relationship and settles to it. Then there is the merchant, and between acquirer and merchant sits the **sub-acquirer/PayFac** (*subcredenciador*) ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/resources/more/credit-card-payment-authorization-and-transaction-settlement-process)). Brazilian regulation defines the subcredenciador as an arrangement participant that enables merchants to accept an instrument but does **not** settle as a creditor to the issuer ([Migalhas](https://www.migalhas.com.br/depeso/277839/banco-central-disciplina-a-figura-do-subcredenciador); [BCB Voto 159/2025](https://normativos.bcb.gov.br/Votos/BCB/2025159/Voto_do_BC_159_2025.pdf)). A Jupiter-like PSP that onboards merchants, receives funds and pays them out is a subcredenciador in Brazilian law. Some role details come from background knowledge and are **unverified** in the notes. On that basis: Cielo, Rede, Getnet, Stone, PagBank and Mercado Pago are acquirers. Pagar.me is Stone's online PSP. Adyen acts as its own acquirer in many markets, while Stripe mostly works as a PayFac on partner acquirers. Stripe's and Adyen's Brazilian license status was not checked.

Authorization takes seconds and moves no money; it only places a hold. Capture turns that hold into a clearing record. Clearing runs in overnight batches. Internationally, settlement happens T+1 to T+3 and merchant funding in 2–3 business days ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/resources/more/credit-card-payment-authorization-and-transaction-settlement-process)). Before clearing, a merchant cancels with an authorization reversal, full or partial. After clearing, a refund is a separate message. In Increase's words: "When a payment is refunded, Visa sends a separate card_refund message" ([Increase](https://increase.com/documentation/card-payment-lifecycle)). Brazil stretches the money leg far more. Under **parcelado lojista** (merchant-financed installments) the merchant receives each installment monthly starting at D+30, net of the MDR for that installment count. Under **parcelado emissor** (issuer-financed) the issuer carries the interest and the merchant gets the full amount at once ([Stone docs](https://online.stone.com.br/docs/tipos-de-transa%C3%A7%C3%B5es); [Finanzero](https://finanzero.com.br/blog/qual-a-diferenca-entre-parcelado-lojista-e-parcelado-emissor/)). The BCB (Banco Central do Brasil) estimates that in credit arrangements the **average time from sale to merchant payout is about two months**, and that **about R$ 480 bn (3.9% of GDP)** is outstanding in credit-card settlement at any moment ([BCB Voto 159/2025](https://normativos.bcb.gov.br/Votos/BCB/2025159/Voto_do_BC_159_2025.pdf)). That float is the economic origin of the *agenda de recebíveis* (a merchant's schedule of future receivables) and of anticipation (*antecipação*). The BCB has said its planned credit-interchange consultation will not change these settlement deadlines ([Finsiders](https://finsidersbrasil.com.br/congresso-abipag-2025/bc-vai-lancar-consulta-publica-para-limitar-tarifas-de-intercambio-de-cartoes/)). Two settlement terms are **unverified** in the notes: D+1 for debit and D+1/D+2 for prepaid.

### Pricing economics

The merchant discount rate (MDR) is the sum of interchange (paid to the issuer), the scheme fee and the acquirer's margin. BCB data for the first half of each year show average **credit MDR falling from 2.32% (2023) to 2.16% (2025)** while **average credit interchange rose from 1.60% to 1.68%**. Debit MDR sat around 1.08%, against debit and prepaid interchange pinned at their caps ([Bahia Econômica citing BCB](https://bahiaeconomica.com.br/wp/2025/11/10/tarifas-de-intercambio-medias-praticadas-no-mercado-de-cartoes-seguem-estaveis/); [BCB open data – MDR](https://dadosabertos.bcb.gov.br/dataset/estatisticas-meios-pagamentos/resource/6d7e8d02-7aa0-48d5-8d4d-ca3dc76d13c7); [BCB open data – TIC](https://dadosabertos.bcb.gov.br/dataset/estatisticas-meios-pagamentos/resource/8c5762e0-82b3-4288-96b0-8734f275265d)). The implied scheme-plus-acquirer slice on credit is therefore **about 0.48 pp and shrinking**. Resolução BCB 246/2022 capped **debit interchange at 0.5% and prepaid at 0.7%** from 1 April 2023 ([FGV](https://regulacaoemnumeros-direitorio.fgv.br/post/banco-central-aprova-resolucao-que-disciplina-tarifa-de-intercambio-de-cartao-de-debito-e-pre); [BCB Estudo Especial 106](https://www.bcb.gov.br/conteudo/relatorioinflacao/EstudosEspeciais/EE106_Resultado_regulatorio_cap_cartao_de_debito.pdf)). The BCB has announced a consultation on capping *credit* interchange, but the notes found no final rule by September 2026 ([Investing.com BR](https://br.investing.com/news/world-news/banco-central-do-brasil-planeja-consulta-publica-sobre-limites-de-tarifas-de-cartao-de-credito-93CH-1567199)). Global reference points: EU interchange caps are 0.2% on debit and 0.3% on credit, the US Durbin cap on regulated debit is $0.21 + 0.05%, and US businesses paid about **US$187.2 bn** in card fees in 2024 ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/resources/more/interchange-fees-101-what-they-are-how-they-work-and-how-to-cut-costs)). Pix costs merchants about **0.22% vs about 2.2% for credit cards** ([Finsiders/EBANX](https://finsidersbrasil.com.br/estudos-e-relatorios/em-2025-pix-ultrapassara-cartao-em-pagamentos-no-e-commerce-diz-ebanx/)). As processing margin thins, the profit pool moves to anticipation, accounts and credit, and for large banks acquiring "deixou de ser negócio por si" ("stopped being a business in itself") ([InfoMoney](https://www.infomoney.com.br/business/nova-batalha-das-maquininhas-mostra-que-para-bancoes-adquirencia-deixou-de-ser-negocio-por-si/); [InvestNews](https://investnews.com.br/negocios/destronada-nas-maquininhas-cielo-luta-para-se-manter-relevante-na-era-do-pix/)). Anticipation rates of 1.99–2.49% per month, with a 2–3.5% market range, come only from **low-quality comparison sites**, and their "BCB data" attribution is unverified ([Calculadora de Taxas](https://www.calculadoradetaxas.com.br/maquininha-com-antecipacao)).

### Market size and share

In 2025 cards moved **R$ 4.5 tn** in Brazil (+10.1%; credit R$ 3.1 tn), across 48.1 bn transactions. Contactless was 73.6% of in-person purchases, and card-not-present volume was R$ 1.1 tn, almost all of it credit ([Panorama Abecs](https://panoramaabecs.com.br/economia-pagamentos-cartoes-brasil-2025-dados-abecs/)). Recurring card payments grew 34% to R$ 141.9 bn ([IstoÉ Dinheiro](https://istoedinheiro.com.br/abecs-pagamentos-recorrentes-com-cartoes-crescem-34-em-2025-a-r-1419-bi-2)). Pix moved **R$ 35.4 tn across 79.8 bn transactions**. That total includes P2P and B2B transfers, so the fair comparison with cards is checkout share. Worldpay puts Brazilian e-commerce in 2025 at Pix 42%, credit 40%, wallets 10% and debit 4%, and projects Pix at 34% of point-of-sale spending rising to 46% by 2030 ([Poder360 / Worldpay GPR 2026](https://www.poder360.com.br/poder-economia/pix-chega-a-42-dos-pagamentos-on-line-no-brasil/)). Acquiring is concentrated. By TPV in January 2026: Rede 18%, Cielo 17%, PagBank 17%, Stone 15%, Mercado Pago 9% and Getnet 5%, with bank-owned acquirers gaining ([Let's Money / UBS BB](https://www.letsmoney.com.br/noticias/rede-itau-lidera-volume-adquirentes/); [NeoFeed](https://neofeed.com.br/negocios/na-disputa-das-adquirentes-rede-do-itau-avanca/en/)). Newer methods include Pix por aproximação (NFC, over the no-redirect journey JSR) since February 2025 and Pix Automático since 16 June 2025. The BCB **dropped plans to regulate "Pix Parcelado"** in December 2025 ([Agência Brasil](https://agenciabrasil.ebc.com.br/economia/noticia/2025-12/apos-adiamentos-banco-central-desiste-de-regular-pix-parcelado)).

## 2025–2026 rules turned every sub-acquirer into a settlement participant

### Licensing, settlement and client funds

Brazil's framework is Lei 12.865/2013, with Res. BCB 80/2021 for payment institution (IP) types and Res. BCB 150/2021 for arrangement rules. It changed sharply in late 2025. Res. BCB 80 lists four IP types: e-money issuer, post-paid instrument issuer, **credenciador**, and payment initiator (ITP). An ITP may not hold client funds ([LegisWeb, Res. BCB 80](https://www.legisweb.com.br/legislacao/?id=411674)). **Res. BCB 494/2025 abolished the volume thresholds** that used to let credenciadores and issuers operate without authorization. The old threshold was R$ 500 mi over 12 months. Unauthorized incumbents had to file between **1 and 31 May 2026** ([LegisWeb Res. 494](https://www.legisweb.com.br/legislacao/?id=483156); [AML Reputacional](https://www.amlreputacional.com.br/2025/11/18/instituicoes-de-pagamento-reguladas-como-se-preparar-diante-da-resolucao-bcb-no-494/)). **Res. BCB 522/2025** amended Res. 150 in two ways. First, every subcredenciador must join centralized settlement (the SLC, run by Núclea) **as both receiver and payer to merchants, regardless of volume**. Schemes had to suspend capture for non-compliant subcredenciadores after **11 May 2026**. Second, the scheme (*instituidor*) must guarantee settlement of every authorized transaction ([LegisWeb Res. 522](https://www.legisweb.com.br/legislacao/?id=486238); [Núclea](https://www.nuclea.com.br/resolucao-bcb-522-2025-subcredenciadoras-slc-nuclea/); [Agência Brasil](https://agenciabrasil.ebc.com.br/economia/noticia/2025-11/bc-bandeira-de-cartao-tera-de-pagar-transacoes-em-caso-de-falhas)). A law-firm summary of Núclea's specs adds the operational details: same-day or D+1 reporting of anticipated settlements, JWS signatures with ICP-Brasil certificates (SLC0912), HTTPS webhooks for asynchronous returns, and at most 1,000 merchant records per message ([NDM Advogados](https://ndmadvogados.com.br/artigo/liquidacao-centralizada-subadquirente/)). These come from a secondary source; the Núclea technical layouts themselves were not read.

Four more rules shape Jupiter's data model. Under the **Lei do Repasse** (Lei 14.031/2020), funds owed to merchants are protected from seizure and from a participant's insolvency ([BCB Voto 159/2025](https://normativos.bcb.gov.br/Votos/BCB/2025159/Voto_do_BC_159_2025.pdf)). Balances in *contas de pagamento* form a **segregated estate** that is separate from the IP's own assets (Lei 12.865 art. 12) ([Planalto](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2011-2014/2013/lei/l12865.htm)). Res. BCB 518/2025 and Res. Conjunta 16/2025 require closing accounts that hide third-party funds and **ban omnibus "contas-bolsão"** in BaaS arrangements, with full BaaS compliance due **31 Dec 2026** ([Silveiro](https://silveiro.com.br/banco-central-reforma-regras-de-capital-e-determina-encerramento-compulsorio-de-contas-bolsao/); [Capital Aberto](https://capitalaberto.com.br/regulamentacao/bc-regulamenta-baas-e-instituicoes-terao-um-ano-para-adequacao/)). Res. Conjunta 14/2025 replaces flat capital minimums with activity-based capital, phased in from June 2026 to January 2028 ([NDM Advogados](https://ndmadvogados.com.br/artigo/mudancas-no-capital-minimo-das-instituicoes-autorizadas/)). Those capital figures come from law-firm summaries. Anti-money-laundering (AML) rules still follow Circular 3.978/2020 as amended by Res. 282/2022 ([BCB](https://normativos.bcb.gov.br/Lists/Normativos/Attachments/50905/Circ_3978_v3_P.pdf)). LGPD, Brazil's data-protection law, applies to registry data, and merchants must opt in before their agenda is disclosed to a financier ([LegisWeb Res. 264](https://www.legisweb.com.br/legislacao/?id=438920)).

On chargebacks, Res. 522 **caps participant liability at disputes opened within 180 days of authorization**. After that, the scheme bears the loss, and chargebacks are barred for disputes caused by a merchant's insolvency ([BCB Voto 159/2025 draft](https://normativos.bcb.gov.br/Votos/BCB/2025159/Voto_do_BC_159_2025.pdf)). The notes read the draft annex, not the final text. An earlier consultation had proposed 120 days ([Finsiders](https://finsidersbrasil.com.br/reportagem-exclusiva-fintechs/novas-regras-de-chargebacks-de-cartoes-de-credito-dividem-bancos-e-bandeiras/)). Consumer law (CDC art. 49) adds a 7-day right of withdrawal for e-commerce purchases ([advocaciareis](https://advocaciareis.adv.br/blog/chargeback/)).

### The receivables registry is a waterfall engine with legal deadlines

Res. CMN 4.734/2019 and Res. BCB 264/2022 (amended many times through Res. 562/2026) made card receivables a standardized, portable asset. A **UR (unidade de recebíveis)** is keyed by four things: the merchant's CPF/CNPJ, the arrangement, the credenciador or subcredenciador, and the settlement date. An **agenda** is the set of URs that share the first three ([LegisWeb Res. 264](https://www.legisweb.com.br/legislacao/?id=438920)). The credenciador must register each UR and **update it by the business day after the sale**. The UR value is gross minus *only* four things: MDR and fees, reversals from cancellation, dispute or fraud, settlements already made, and blocks. Pre-contracted anticipation is registered on the anticipated date. Financiers negotiate URs by fixed value or by percentage. Blocks (risk reserves, offsets for chargebacks already settled) cannot be negotiated and cannot reduce amounts already committed to contracts. Reconciliation runs on three schedules, **daily** (constituted values and effects), **weekly** (settlement amounts and domiciles) and **fortnightly** (the list of merchants with active contracts), and any inconsistency must be fixed within **2 business days**. Registry contestations must be answered within 3 business days ([LegisWeb Res. 264](https://www.legisweb.com.br/legislacao/?id=438920)). Registration is **free for credenciadores and subcredenciadores**. Interoperability tariffs between registries are capped per event by Res. 472/2025, for example R$ 0.030055 per agenda event from July 2026, falling to R$ 0.008335 in 2029 ([BCB Convenção PDF](https://www.bcb.gov.br/content/estabilidadefinanceira/spb_docs/convencoes/Conven%C3%A7%C3%A3o%20entre%20Entidades%20Registradoras%20-%20Receb%C3%ADveis%20de%20Arranjos%20de%20Pagamento.pdf)).

The multilateral **Convenção entre Entidades Registradoras** makes this algorithmic. A credenciador connects to **one registry at a time**, and financiers may connect to several. There are two effect types: **ownership transfer** (*troca de titularidade*) and **lien** (*ônus*, which covers collateralized credit, *trava*/promise of assignment, and court seizures). Settlement is **FIFO** across effects in order of contract acceptance, with the free portion paid last. Reductions such as a chargeback consume **the free portion first, then contracts LIFO**. A contract spanning several credenciadores is split proportionally over the whole agenda. Settlement must be reported to the registry by the next business day ([Convenção, CERC copy](https://www.cerc.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/convencao_entre_entidades_registradoras_recebiveis_de_arranjos_de_pagamento.pdf)). Anticipation comes in two forms. Pre-contracted anticipation moves the settlement date. Post-contracted anticipation pays constituted amounts early, must honor effects already in place, and cannot touch blocked amounts. Since 4.734, merchants can take either from any institution, including non-financial ones ([Equals](https://equals.com.br/en/blog/resolucao-4734-2019-e-circular-bacen-3952-2019-na-antecipacao-de-recebiveis/)). There is one caveat. Res. BCB 514/2025 set 2-business-day deadlines for lifting liens and cancelling pre-contracted anticipation, but **Res. BCB 562/2026 revoked and replaced that article** from 11 May 2026, and the notes could not read the new wording ([LegisWeb Res. 562](https://www.legisweb.com.br/legislacao/?id=495271)). The registries are generally named as CERC, CIP/Núclea, TAG and B3. One market-structure source lists only the first three ([Núclea](https://www.nuclea.com.br/registradora-de-recebiveis-de-cartoes/)).

## Pix and boleto are specified to the byte in public documents

### Pix: identifiers, API, security and lifecycle

Pix settles on the BCB's real-time gross settlement system (SPI), which carries ISO 20022 messages: pacs.008 for payments, pacs.004 for returns and pacs.002 for status. Keys resolve through DICT, the key directory. Several identifiers are fixed-format. The **endToEndId** is 32 characters: `E` + an 8-character ISPB (the participant code) + `yyyyMMddHHmm` in UTC + 11 alphanumerics ([Cielo Pix manual](https://developercielo.github.io/en/manual/apipix); [pix-api openapi.yaml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bacen/pix-api/master/openapi.yaml)). The **txid** for a charge is 26–35 alphanumerics and must be unique per receiver CPF/CNPJ **forever, even after cancellation**. In a static QR code it is capped at 25 characters, or `***` when absent ([Manual de Padrões v2.10.0](https://www.bcb.gov.br/content/estabilidadefinanceira/pix/Regulamento_Pix/II_ManualdePadroesparaIniciacaoDoPix.pdf)). The **idRec** for Pix Automático is 29 characters, prefixed `RR` when retries are allowed and `RN` when they are not. Since pix-api 2.9.0, ISPB, CNPJ and idRec may be **alphanumeric**, which matches the Receita Federal's move to alphanumeric CNPJs from **31 July 2026** ([changelog](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bacen/pix-api/master/changelog.md); [Receita Federal](https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/pt-br/assuntos/noticias/2026/julho/implantacao-do-cnpj-alfanumerico-ocorrera-a-partir-de-31-de-julho)). Amounts are strings with two decimals, matching the pattern `\d{1,10}\.\d{2}`.

The **bacen/pix-api OpenAPI spec is at v2.10.0**, kept in step with Manual de Padrões v2.10.0 of 19 August 2026. It defines these resources ([openapi.yaml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bacen/pix-api/master/openapi.yaml)):

- charges: `/cob`, `/cobv` (with due date) and `/lotecobv` (asynchronous batch, returns 202);
- payload locations: `/loc` and `/locrec`;
- payments and returns: `/pix/{e2eid}` and `/pix/{e2eid}/devolucao/{id}`, where the devolução `id` is client-generated;
- webhooks registered per key: `/webhook/{chave}`, `/webhookrec` and `/webhookcobr`;
- Pix Automático: `rec`, `solicrec` and `cobr`, added in 2.7.0.

Security is strict. PSPs must use TLS ≥1.2 and OAuth2 client credentials over **mTLS with certificate-bound access tokens (RFC 8705)**, and the resource server checks that the token's certificate thumbprint matches the TLS client certificate ([Manual de Padrões Anexo II](https://www.bcb.gov.br/content/estabilidadefinanceira/pix/Regulamento_Pix/II_ManualdePadroesparaIniciacaoDoPix.pdf)). Errors follow RFC 7807, with typed URIs under `https://pix.bcb.gov.br/api/v2/error/`. A `cob` has statuses `ATIVA → CONCLUIDA | REMOVIDA_PELO_USUARIO_RECEBEDOR | REMOVIDA_PELO_PSP`. **Expiry is not a status**: it is derived from `criacao + expiracao`, which defaults to 86,400 s. The spec prose says `REMOVIDO_…` while the enum says `REMOVIDA_…`, so implement the enum. A devolução moves `EM_PROCESSAMENTO → DEVOLVIDO | NAO_REALIZADO`, and its nature enum includes `MED_FRAUDE` and `MED_PIX_AUTOMATICO`.

The **BR Code** (the Pix QR payload) is EMV MPM TLV with GUI `br.gov.bcb.pix`. A static code carries the key. A dynamic code carries a URL of at most 77 characters, without scheme, that resolves to a JWS-signed payload; fields 54 (amount) and 62-05 (txid) are ignored in dynamic codes. A "composto" code adds a recurrence URL in template 80–99 on the same domain. The string ends with a **CRC-16/CCITT-FALSE** (polynomial 0x1021, initial value 0xFFFF) computed over everything up to and including `6304`. The research notes reproduced the manual's example checksum `1D3D` in code, so it can serve as a unit-test fixture ([Manual de Padrões §2.6.3](https://www.bcb.gov.br/content/estabilidadefinanceira/pix/Regulamento_Pix/II_ManualdePadroesparaIniciacaoDoPix.pdf)). "Pix Copia e Cola" is the same string as text.

Fraud and recurrence add stateful flows. **MED 2.0 has been mandatory since 2 Feb 2026**. Returns can trace funds across intermediate accounts, suspected funds are blocked for **up to 11 days**, the originating institution must notify the receiving one **within 30 minutes**, and contestation inside the banking app is mandatory ([Agência Brasil](https://agenciabrasil.ebc.com.br/economia/noticia/2026-02/novas-regras-de-seguranca-do-pix-entram-em-vigor-veja-mudancas); [Transfeera](https://transfeera.com/blog/med-pix-2-0/)). From 1 Sep 2026 (IN BCB 766/2026), receivers have **80 days instead of 30** to contest a MED return ([meutudo](https://meutudo.com.br/blog/noticias/2026/09/02/prazo-para-contestacao-de-pix-devolvido-pelo-med-passa-de-30-para-80-dias-entenda)). **Pix Automático** has four authorization journeys; only journey 1, a push request through `solicrec`, can be rejected. Charges (`cobr`) must reach the payer's PSP **2–10 days before the due date**. Retry policy is either `NAO_PERMITE` (none) or `PERMITE_3R_7D`, meaning up to 3 retries on different days within 7 days. Each retry gets a new endToEndId ([Manual Anexo IV](https://www.bcb.gov.br/content/estabilidadefinanceira/pix/Regulamento_Pix/II_ManualdePadroesparaIniciacaoDoPix.pdf); [BCB FAQ](https://www.bcb.gov.br/content/estabilidadefinanceira/pix/pix-automatico-FAQ-participantes.pdf)). The R$500 per-transaction cap on Pix por aproximação (JSR) is **removed from 1 Oct 2026** ([Mix Vale](https://www.mixvale.com.br/2026/09/25/pix-por-aproximacao-perde-limite-de-r-500-em-outubro/)).

### Boleto and CNAB 240

A collection boleto's barcode has **44 digits**:

| Positions | Content |
|---|---|
| 1–3 | bank |
| 4 | currency |
| 5 | general check digit (mod 11) |
| 6–9 | due-date factor |
| 10–19 | amount in cents |
| 20–44 | bank-defined free field |

The 47-digit *linha digitável* (the typed line) rearranges these into five fields, three of which carry their own mod-10 check digit ([FEBRABAN layout](https://cmsarquivos.febraban.org.br/Arquivos/documentos/PDF/Layout%20-%20C%C3%B3digo%20de%20Barras%20ATUALIZADO.pdf); [Efí](https://sejaefi.com.br/blog/campos-dos-boletos-linha-digitavel)). The research notes verified the check-digit and linha algorithms empirically against a real Bradesco barcode rather than quoting a FEBRABAN collection spec. The due-date factor counts days from 07/10/1997. It **hit 9999 on 21 Feb 2025 and restarted at 1000 on 22 Feb 2025**, so decoders must resolve the ambiguity, and FEBRABAN publishes no rule for doing so ([KMEE](https://kmee.com.br/blog/mudanca-fator-vencimento-boletos-2025/); [Senior](https://documentacao.senior.com.br/noticias/2025/2025-01-29-fator-vencimento-febraban.htm)). All boletos are registered in Núclea's centralized platform (Nova Plataforma de Cobrança). The "boleto híbrido" adds a Pix QR, usually a `cobv` ([FEBRABAN NPC](https://portal.febraban.org.br/pagina/3150/1094/pt-br/servicos-novo-plataforma-boletos)).

**CNAB 240 v10.11** is a file format with fixed 240-character lines ([FEBRABAN CNAB 240 v10.11](https://cmsarquivos.febraban.org.br/Arquivos/documentos/PDF/Layout%20padrao%20CNAB240%20V%2010%2011%20-%2021_08_2023.pdf)):

- **Structure:** file header (record type 0), then batches (1 header, 3 details, 5 trailer), then a file trailer (9). A collection batch uses batch layout `060` inside file layout `103`.
- **Segments:** remittance files require segments **P+Q** (title and payer), and return files require **T+U** (status and amounts). U carries amount paid at positions 78–92, net credited at 93–107 and credit date at 146–153.
- **Codes:** remittance movement codes (C004) include 01 register, 02 cancel and **61 add Pix QR**. Return codes (C044) map to domain events: 02 confirmed, 03 rejected, 06 paid, 09 written off, 17 paid after write-off, 28 fee. Reason group C records the payment channel, where **61 = paid via Pix**.

Banks publish their own variants of the 240 layout. CNAB 400 is bank-specific and has no FEBRABAN standard.

## Card rails remain ISO 8583 underneath 3DS, tokens and dispute programs

**ISO 8583** is still the production authorization protocol. ISO 20022's card messages (ATICA) have **no migration deadline** ([Tapix](https://www.tapix.io/resources/post/iso-8583-vs-iso-20022)). A message has an MTI, bitmaps and data elements (DE2 PAN, DE4 amount, DE11 STAN, DE39 response code, DE55 chip data) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_8583)). The failure semantics matter most. *Requests* are end-to-end with timeouts and automatic reversals. *Advices* are store-and-forward and repeat (0421) until acknowledged. When a response times out, the acquirer sends a 0420 reversal ([LinkedIn: ISO 8583 message flow](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/whats-iso-8583-message-flow-part-two-ahmed-el-nazer)). Stand-in processing, full response-code tables and scheme private fields (Visa F62/F126, Mastercard DE48, installment data in DE112) are **unverified or gated** in the notes. The notes flag one case as a test Jupiter must include: a late 0110 that arrives after a 0420 was sent.

Authorization lifetimes depend on context. Under Visa's April 2024 framework, holds last **5 days card-present, 10 days for customer-initiated card-not-present (CNP), 5 days for merchant-initiated transactions (MIT), and 30 days for lodging and car-rental estimates**. The Extended Authorization Service allows up to 30 days for 0.08%. Incremental authorizations add amount but do not extend Visa validity ([Chargebacks911](https://chargebacks911.com/visa-authorization-rules/); [Visa PDF](https://corporate.visa.com/content/dam/VCOM/regional/na/us/support-legal/documents/authorization-framework-will-be-updated-to-simplify-authorization-processing-time-frames.pdf); [Checkout.com](https://www.checkout.com/docs/payments/manage-payments/authorize-a-payment/adjust-an-estimated-authorization)). **These figures conflict** with Stripe's table of 7 days for Visa customer-initiated CNP, with an operational window of "4 days and 18 hours" ([Stripe docs](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/place-a-hold-on-a-payment-method)). Stripe likely applies a more conservative policy of its own, so Jupiter should treat validity as configurable data. Rules for chaining stored credentials, where each MIT carries the network transaction ID from the first customer-initiated transaction (CIT), are well known but **unverified** in the notes.

**EMV 3DS 2.1.0 was retired in September 2024**. The baseline is now 2.2.0, and 2.3.1 is the latest spec ([Cybersource](https://developer.cybersource.com/docs/cybs/en-us/mandates/relnote/all/na/mandates-april-2024/mandates-payments-intro-april-2024/3ds-2-1-0-sunset.html); [GPayments](https://www.gpayments.com/blog/article/evolving-security-an-overview-of-3d-secure-2-3-enhancements/)). The notes marked the following protocol details **unverified**, since EMVCo PDFs were not fetched: the AReq/ARes/CReq/CRes/RReq flow, `transStatus` values, ECI codes, the CAVV cryptogram, and liability shift. **Network tokenization** is mainstream: Visa Token Service has issued more than 12.6 bn tokens, and Mastercard has committed to 100% European e-commerce tokenization by 2030 ([Glenbrook](https://glenbrook.com/payments_views/we-really-cant-stop-talking-about-tokenization-a-2025-update/); [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/mastercard/2024/mastercard-new-use-cases-prep-for-total-ecommerce-tokenization-by-2030/)). **PCI DSS v4.0.1** requirements dated for the future became mandatory on 31 March 2025. The January 2025 SAQ A dropped the script requirements 6.4.3 and 11.6.1 but added an eligibility test that the site is "not susceptible to attacks from scripts" ([PCI SSC blog](https://blog.pcisecuritystandards.org/important-updates-announced-for-merchants-validating-to-self-assessment-questionnaire-a)). Truncation may show at most the first 8 and last 4 digits, but **first 6 and last 4 is the only format every brand accepts**. If both truncated and hashed PANs are stored, they must not be correlatable ([PCI SSC](https://blog.pcisecuritystandards.org/8-digit-bins-and-pci-dss-what-you-need-to-know); [Control Gap](https://www.controlgap.com/blog/new-bin-ranges-and-pci-truncation)).

Disputes have become a monitored metric. Visa's **VAMP** ratio, effective 1 June 2025, divides fraud reports (TC40) plus disputes (TC15) by settled CNP transactions. An acquirer is "Excessive" at ≥70 bps. A merchant is "Excessive" at 220 bps, falling to 150 bps in the US, EU, Asia-Pacific and Canada from April 2026, and Latin America is already at 150 bps. An enumeration (card-testing) ratio of ≥20% is also monitored. **Programs for Brazil "will be announced later"** ([Visa VAMP fact sheet](https://corporate.visa.com/content/dam/VCOM/corporate/visa-perspectives/security-and-trust/documents/visa-acquirer-monitoring-program-fact-sheet-2025.pdf)). Mastercard gives about 120 days to file, 45 days to represent, and 30 + 30 days for pre-arbitration ([Chargebacks911](https://chargebacks911.com/chargeback-rules/chargeback-time-limits/mastercard-chargeback-time-limit/)). Under Visa's Compelling Evidence 3.0, a 10.4 fraud dispute can be defeated with two prior undisputed transactions that settled more than 120 days earlier ([Checkout.com](https://www.checkout.com/blog/visa-compelling-evidence-3-0)). On risk scoring, Stripe Radar runs a DNN over more than 1,000 features in under 100 ms against a base fraud rate of about 1 in 1,000, learning from labels (disputes, fraud reports) that arrive late ([Stripe](https://stripe.dev/blog/how-we-built-it-stripe-radar)). Since 2025, rules can combine the model score with the issuer's CVC and postal-code check results ([Stripe blog](https://stripe.com/blog/using-ai-dynamic-radar-rules)).

## Stripe supplies the API grammar; Brazilian PSPs add split, receivables and anticipation

Stripe's design is the reference, and its **`/v2` namespace fixes several v1 choices** ([API v2 overview](https://docs.stripe.com/api-v2-overview)):

- **Payment state:** the PaymentIntent and SetupIntent state machine is `requires_payment_method → requires_confirmation → requires_action → processing → succeeded | canceled`, plus `requires_capture`. A decline returns the intent to `requires_payment_method`, so one intent holds many attempts ([Stripe lifecycle](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/paymentintents/lifecycle)).
- **Balance ledger:** the BalanceTransaction records gross, fee and net, plus `available_on` and `reporting_category` ([BalanceTransaction](https://docs.stripe.com/api/balance_transactions/object)).
- **Idempotency:** v1 caches the first response for at least 24 h, *including 500s*. v2 **retries a failed request without side effects** within a 30-day window.
- **Versioning:** versions are dates, pinned per account, with backward transformer modules. Since 2024 there is a monthly non-breaking release and two breaking releases a year; the current version is `2026-08-26.dahlia` ([Versioning](https://docs.stripe.com/api/versioning); [Stripe blog](https://stripe.com/blog/api-versioning)).
- **Pagination and expansion:** v1 uses cursors (`starting_after`); v2 uses page tokens and replaces `expand` with `include`.
- **Webhooks:** the header is `Stripe-Signature: t=…,v1=HMAC-SHA256("{t}.{body}")` with a 5-minute tolerance, retries run for up to 3 days, delivery order is not guaranteed, and receivers deduplicate by event id. All v2 events are **thin**: the handler fetches the latest state instead of trusting a snapshot ([Webhooks](https://docs.stripe.com/webhooks)).
- **Errors:** errors are typed, with `decline_code` and `advice_code`, and 402 is used for declines ([Errors](https://docs.stripe.com/api/errors)).

Stripe's Brazil coverage is thin. **Brazilian accounts get one-time Pix only, and "Pix Automático isn't available in Brazil"**. Boleto has **no refunds and no disputes**, and settles T+2 ([Stripe Pix](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/pix); [Stripe boleto](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/boleto)). Adyen models every modification (capture, cancel, refund, reversal, authorization adjustment) as asynchronous. The response returns `received` with a new `pspReference`, and the outcome arrives by webhook with `originalReference` pointing to the original payment. Platform splits are typed booking lines: `BalanceAccount`, `Commission`, `PaymentFee`, `Remainder` ([Adyen capture](https://docs.adyen.com/online-payments/capture/); [split at authorization](https://docs.adyen.com/marketplaces/split-transactions/split-payments-at-authorization)).

Brazilian PSPs share a vocabulary Stripe lacks: recipient, split rule, receivable, anticipation and transfer settings.

| PSP | Central object | Split model | Notable semantics |

Recipients carry `transfer_settings` and `automatic_anticipation_settings` ([split](https://docs.pagar.me/reference/split-1); [chargeback](https://docs.pagar.me/page/chargeback-novo-status-na-cobran%C3%A7a); [anticipation](https://docs.pagar.me/reference/informa%C3%A7%C3%B5es-de-antecipa%C3%A7%C3%A3o-autom%C3%A1tica-1)) |

Pagar.me's diverging Order and Charge statuses and Iugu's silent 100% rule are the examples to avoid.

## Every mature ledger converges on append-only, zero-sum entries

The public writeups agree. **Stripe's Ledger** is immutable ("Transactions previously published into Ledger cannot be deleted or modified"). It models producer systems as state machines and treats **clearing accounts that must return to zero** as a correctness signal: "A single missing, late, or incorrect transaction immediately creates a detectable accuracy issue". It processes 5 bn events per day and reports more than 99.9999% explainability of money movement ([Stripe Ledger](https://stripe.dev/blog/ledger-stripe-system-for-tracking-and-validating-money-movement)). **Square Books** uses three tables (books, journal entries, book entries). Entries are append-only and sum to zero, and the only mutable values are the books' running balances ([Square](https://developer.squareup.com/blog/books-an-immutable-double-entry-accounting-database-service/)). **Uber** validates zero-sum before commit, never modifies a money order (adjustments are new orders), and keeps an entity changelog for more than 1.2 bn entities ([Uber](https://www.uber.com/us/en/blog/ubers-payments-platform/)). **Modern Treasury** caches balances as pending and posted, and it **disables reads on accounts whose cached balance drifts** from the entries ([MT Part VI](https://www.moderntreasury.com/journal/how-to-scale-a-ledger-part-vi)). **TigerBeetle** keeps four balance fields per account (`debits_pending`, `debits_posted`, `credits_pending`, `credits_posted`) and uses **two-phase transfers** that post, void or expire. These map one-to-one onto authorize, capture and void ([TigerBeetle two-phase](https://docs.tigerbeetle.com/coding/two-phase-transfers/)).

Hot accounts are the known scaling trap. Optimistic version locking **starves**: "some Transactions will never be able to commit" ([MT Part IV](https://www.moderntreasury.com/journal/how-to-scale-a-ledger-part-iv)). The published remedies are three. Lock only the user-side accounts and post to the hot account asynchronously ([MT docs](https://docs.moderntreasury.com/ledgers/docs/handle-concurrency)). Batch writes to the hot entity, which gave Uber a 10x gain ([Uber](https://www.uber.com/us/en/blog/ubers-payments-platform/)). Or serialize everything on a single core with large batches, as TigerBeetle does. Sharded sub-balances are folklore here: no source in the notes describes them.

For idempotency, Brandur Leach's design is canonical. A key table has a unique `(user_id, key)` constraint plus `locked_at`, `request_params`, `recovery_point` and the stored response. Work runs in "atomic phases" separated by foreign calls, with a **completer** that pushes abandoned requests forward and a **reaper** that deletes keys after about 72 h ([brandur.org](https://brandur.org/idempotency-keys)). Airbnb's Orpheus library reads and writes the idempotency record on the primary database to avoid duplicates caused by replica lag. The notes took this from snippets, because the post returned 403 ([Airbnb](https://medium.com/airbnb-engineering/avoiding-double-payments-in-a-distributed-payments-system-2981f6b070bb)). Shopify recommends ULIDs over UUIDv4 (50% faster inserts), 1 s connect and 5 s read timeouts (Go's HTTP client sets no timeouts by default), and reconciliation that records anomalies and fixes them automatically ([Shopify](https://shopify.engineering/building-resilient-payment-systems)). On **unknown outcomes**, every source says the same thing: a timeout is neither a success nor a failure. Record intent before the foreign call, then resolve the outcome by idempotent retry, a status query or a completer. Uber runs synchronous payment flows on Cadence ([Uber](https://www.uber.com/us/en/blog/ubers-payments-platform/)), and Coinbase moved its transaction flows to Temporal ([Temporal](https://temporal.io/resources/case-studies/coinbase)). For correctness, the strongest public practice is continuous invariant checking combined with **deterministic simulation testing**. TigerBeetle's VOPR simulator injects faults "at 1000x speed", and Jepsen has analyzed TigerBeetle independently ([TigerBeetle docs](https://docs.tigerbeetle.com/single-page/); [Jepsen](https://jepsen.io/analyses/tigerbeetle-0.16.11)). No payments-specific TLA+ writeup and no three-way reconciliation algorithm was found. Go has proven itself at payments scale: Monzo's whole bank runs on Go microservices, and Mercado Libre serves 8–10 M requests per minute at under 10 ms on Go ([Monzo](https://monzo.com/blog/2016/09/19/building-a-modern-bank-backend); [go.dev](https://go.dev/solutions/mercadolibre)).

## Implications for Jupiter

### Concrete design consequences

1. **One payment intent, many rail sub-state machines.** Use Stripe's intent states as the universal core. Pix and boleto sit in `requires_action`, with a QR code or voucher in `next_action`, until a webhook or return file moves them to `succeeded` or `canceled`. Cards follow `authorized → captured → settled`. A decline returns the intent to `requires_payment_method` and a new attempt is recorded. Add an explicit **`unknown`/`pending_confirmation`** attempt state, resolved by status query, webhook or a completer, and never treat a timeout as a failure.
2. **Own a Postgres double-entry ledger with TigerBeetle's semantics.** Use `accounts / transactions / entries` tables. Enforce sum-to-zero in the database. Keep pending and posted balances. Map authorization to a pending transfer, capture to post (full or partial), and void or expiry to void. Reversals are new transactions. Store money as `int64` minor units plus currency, split with largest-remainder allocation, and use decimals only for rates. Keep separate ledger books for client funds and Jupiter's own funds, because segregation (Lei 12.865 art. 12) and the conta-bolsão ban forbid omnibus designs.
3. **Make invariants a product feature.** Run a nightly job that checks zero-sum per transaction, recomputes cached balances from entries (disabling reads on drift, as Modern Treasury does), and verifies that clearing accounts reach zero (Stripe's clearing metric). Back it with property-based tests and a seeded, deterministic simulation harness that injects timeouts, duplicate and late webhooks, and late 0110 responses after a 0420: a "mini-VOPR".
4. **Hot accounts.** Lock merchant and customer accounts synchronously. Route platform, fee and settlement accounts through an asynchronous batched balance applier. Entries are still written synchronously, so the invariant holds.
5. **Idempotency done right.** Implement Brandur's key table with recovery points and atomic phases, plus a completer and a reaper. Adopt Stripe v2 semantics: a failed request retries without side effects, and a parameter mismatch returns 409 or 422. Use ULID or UUIDv7 identifiers. **Propagate idempotency downstream deterministically**: Pix `txid` (26–35 characters, never reused), devolução `id`, and ISO 8583 STAN/RRN.
6. **Receivables are a first-class domain, and the chief differentiator from Stripe.** At capture, generate N URs per installment at D+30·k, net of per-installment MDR, keyed by (merchant document, arrangement code, subcredenciador CNPJ, settlement date). Model `ContractEffect{OWNERSHIP_TRANSFER|LIEN, beneficiary, FIXED|PERCENT, value, domicile, accepted_at}`, settle FIFO over effects then the free portion, and reduce from the free portion first, then LIFO over effects. Blocks cannot touch committed amounts. Behind a `RegistryAdapter`, schedule D+1 updates, settlement notices, contestation SLAs (3 and 5 business days), opt-in-gated agenda queries, and daily, weekly and fortnightly reconciliation with a 2-business-day fix window.
7. **Anticipation as `simulate → create`.** Follow Asaas and Pagar.me. Price as PV = Σ installmentᵢ / (1 + r)^(daysᵢ/30), check free value against the registry first, and report anticipations to the simulated Núclea SLC the same day or at D+1.
8. **Split as typed ledger lines with explicit liability.** Use Adyen-style lines (`recipient`, `commission`, `fee`, `remainder`) plus Pagar.me-style invariants: exactly one party is liable for chargebacks and one bears the remainder. Validate that splits never exceed the net amount, and never repeat Iugu's silent 100% failure.
9. **A single disputes model for card chargebacks and Pix MED.** Use per-scheme deadline tables (Mastercard 120/45/30/30), a configurable **180-day Brazilian liability cap**, and MED 2.0 flows (30-minute notification, block for up to 11 days, 80-day contestation). Debit future URs as well as balances. Keep fraud-report labels (TC40-like) separate from disputes, and compute a VAMP-style ratio and a card-testing enumeration ratio as monitored metrics.
10. **Card authorization expiry is data, not code.** Compute `expires_at` from scheme, card-present or not, CIT or MIT, estimated flag and MCC. Store `network_transaction_id` from the first CIT and send it on every MIT. Carry `installments{count, MERCHANT|ISSUER}` on the authorization.
11. **Vault and PCI posture.** Run the vault as its own service: AES-256-GCM per-record data keys wrapped by a KMS key-encryption key, random tokens, a keyed HMAC fingerprint that cannot be correlated with truncated forms, a BIN8 + last4 display, and CVV held only in memory. Hosted fields keep merchants in SAQ A scope.
12. **API conventions (copy Stripe v2).** Prefixed IDs, `livemode`, JSON bodies, a date-named `Jupiter-Version` header pinned per account with response transformers, page tokens, `include[]`, thin events, `t=…,v1=HMAC` webhook signatures with 5-minute tolerance and 24 h secret rotation, and typed errors carrying `request_id`. Surface rail identifiers (`txid`, `end_to_end_id`, `nosso_numero`, RRN) in `payment_method_details` for reconciliation.
13. **Regulatory posture as configuration.** Model Jupiter as a subcredenciador that participates in SLC as both receiver and payer, not as an entity exempt below R$ 500 mi, which is now outdated. Validate that debit and prepaid interchange passthrough stays within 0.5% and 0.7%. Store CNPJ as an alphanumeric **string**. Add KYC/KYB, sanctions screening (watchman), an account-closure workflow (Res. 518) and 10-year retention.
14. **Infrastructure.** Use pgx + sqlc, River enqueued in the same transaction as each state change (a built-in outbox for webhooks, payouts and CNAB files), OpenTelemetry and testcontainers. Limit Temporal to one showcase flow, such as dispute deadlines, and document the trade-off.

### Open-source gaps Jupiter can credibly fill

The survey found no maintained project in any of six areas, and each is an honest contribution claim for Jupiter:

- a maintained **Go CNAB 240 (and per-bank 400) library** with a remittance writer, return parser and fuzz tests;
- an **SPI/DICT/PSP simulator** that covers MED 2.0 and Pix Automático (rec, solicrec, cobr, 3R_7D retries);
- an **open 3DS2 DS/ACS simulator**;
- a **Go card network and issuer simulator** built on moov-io/iso8583;
- a **receivables-registry and SLC simulator** that implements the Convenção waterfall;
- an **integrated Brazilian PSP reference** combining split, Pix, boleto, card installments, receivables and anticipation on one audited ledger.

A small "awesome-pagamentos-br" list in the README would also be new, since none exists.

## Gaps and open questions

Some regulatory primary texts were not read: the final Res. BCB 522/2025, Res. 530/2025 and 562/2026, Res. Conjunta 14/2025, the Regulamento Pix, Res. 402/2024, the MED 2.0 resolution, and IN 766/2026. It is also unconfirmed whether a large subcredenciador now needs authorization as a credenciador, and whether prepaid arrangements now require UR registration. Several technical layouts were not fetched: CERC, Núclea and TAG registry APIs, Núclea SLC message specs, the SPI message catalogue, the DICT operational manual (key limits, portability, rate limits), the Manual de Segurança do SFN (JWS algorithm and headers for payload locations), CNAB segments R and S, per-bank free-field formats, and CNAB 400. Several card facts are gated in scheme manuals: Brazilian installment fields in ISO 8583, Elo and Hipercard rules and reason codes, Brazilian domestic dispute timeframes, Mastercard's authorization validity table, and chargeback fees. The status of Brazil's credit-interchange cap consultation in 2026 is unknown. So are Visa's VAMP program for Brazil, the go-live date of the D+0 boleto CMP project, and Elo network tokenization. Engineering sources left three holes: no published hot-account throughput on relational databases, no three-way reconciliation algorithm, and no payments-specific TLA+ writeup. Mercado Pago's newer Orders and disbursement APIs, Pagar.me's anticipation and transfer endpoints, and the full enums for Pagar.me Order and Charge and Asaas cobrança were not retrieved.

## Conclusion

The research reverses a common framing. The hard part of a Brazilian PSP is not accepting payments; Pix and card authorization are well specified and increasingly commoditized. The hard part is **the months between capture and payout**. In that window each installment is a registered asset, and financiers, courts and chargebacks compete for it under FIFO/LIFO rules with legal deadlines. Since May 2026, sub-acquirers must also run that window through centralized settlement. Global PSP designs, Stripe included, stop at the balance ledger. A Jupiter whose ledger natively represents receivable units, contract effects and anticipation, and proves its correctness with continuous invariants, would show something neither Stripe's public API nor any open-source project currently shows.

That also sets the order of work. The ledger and idempotency core come first, because every rail depends on them. The receivables and registry domain comes next, as the central differentiator. Pix (spec-generated, including Pix Automático and MED 2.0) and CNAB follow, because the rails are public and the Go tooling gap is real. Cards come last, built on moov-io/iso8583 with in-house 3DS2 and network simulators. Leading with the simulators, and treating every unverified rule above as configuration rather than code, keeps the project accurate as Brazilian regulation continues to change quarter by quarter.
